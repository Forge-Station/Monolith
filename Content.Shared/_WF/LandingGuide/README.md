# Landing guide

Shows a pilot what is under the hull while it flies a planet's air, so they can pick a spot to set down. Whenever the
local player holds a helm (a shuttle console or the cockpit) of a grid on an air layer, or in the gap between two
layers above the ground, every ground tile under the hull is filled over the ship: green for clear ground, red for
something that would stop the landing, amber where no ground is known. A thin line traces the hull's outline and a
line above it sums up ("Landing: clear", "Landing: 3 blocked"). Nothing shows in space, in orbit or on the ground.
**J** (`WFToggleLandingGuide`, rebindable under Shuttle in the controls) turns it off and on for the session.

Red is what a gentle set-down refuses, anything hard and impassable anchored on the ground tile (walls, rock and the
like), plus another grid parked there, which a hull coming down crushes. Amber is a tile with no ground:
not loaded yet, outside a bounded world's circle or a hole such as a cavern mouth.

Entry points: `WFLandingSurveySystem` (shared: which ground tiles are under a hull and what each holds; the server's
set-down refusal, `CEZLevelsSystem.WfObstacleUnder`, asks the same system, so the two agree), `WFLandingGuideSystem`
(client: who is piloting what, survey at most four times a second or when the hull moves a tile or turns, per-tile
readings reused for half a second) and `WFLandingGuideOverlay` (the drawing).

The client reads the ground itself: a pilot's z-level eyes put the ground layer under them in PVS, so its tiles and
anchored entities are already there. It draws on the pilot's own layer at the ground tiles' world position, because
the hull would hide anything drawn in the ground's own pass beneath it.
