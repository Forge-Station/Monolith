# PlanetScan

Worlds on the radar out in space. The shuttle console's radar, radar consoles and the shuttle map draw each body of the
star system as the sector shows it, through its own planet shader (`WFPlanetScopeSprites`), lit from where the star
lies on the plot. A body's scope radius is 300 m per Earth radius, short of its gravity well's capture line (35% of
the surface's `orbitRange`), and never under 12 pixels on the radar or 10 on the map. A world on the scope no longer
carries a name, a blip or a signal tag; clicking it still opens its signal list. A world off the scope keeps its rim
blip and name.

Pointing at a world opens the planet scan (`WFPlanetScanner`), built like the cockpit's asteroid scanner: brackets lock
on and a callout types out the world's name and standing, range to its limb, climate, gravity, air pressure and
whether it is breathable, temperature, hazards, known sites by kind, the clear landing zone round its centre and how
many ships and people are on it. `WFPlanetScanReader` (shared, so the server can test it) reads all of this from what
the client already has: the star system, the body's `WFSectorPlanetComponent` and `WFPlanetSignalsComponent`, the
orbit layer's `WFPlanetMapComponent` (maps reach every client) and the surface and ground prototypes. Hazards are the
surface's `hazards` list plus what the air, heat, gravity and sanction give away; `climate` is a short line on the
surface prototype.
