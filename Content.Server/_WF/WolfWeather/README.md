# WolfWeather

Rain, snow, storms, ash, sand and fog drawn by one shader, stopped by roofs, with wind that pushes loose items,
weather-tinted map light and looping sounds listed on each weather. Storms flash and strike lightning that shocks whoever
it hits. A weather can copy another with `parent` and change single layers with `layerOverrides`, as the blood weathers
do. Players set the detail under Options, Graphics, Weather detail (`wf.wolf_weather.quality`).

Planets: WolfWeather is how planet weather looks, sounds, blows and strikes. `WFPlanetWeatherSystem` still schedules
the stock `weather` prototypes, which name the weather for the timepiece, reports and admin panel; each world's
`wfPlanetWeather` maps them to wolfWeathers with `wolfWeathers`, and the ground layer gets that state, open-ended so
each storm phase cross-fades into the next. A map with WolfWeather state never draws the stock overlay or plays its
loop. Weather falls on a surface only (`IsSurface`): caverns, cellars, the air above and the orbit get none, and
`SetWeather` refuses them. A viewer below the ground sees none even with the ground drawn above them. On a planet roofs decide what is open, and a vessel is under
cover throughout, as for stock weather.

Elsewhere admins use `wolfweather <map> <weather|clear> [seconds]` and `wolfweather_schedule` (Fun flag). A schedule
(`WeatherSchedule` component, `weatherSchedule` prototypes such as `WFTemperate`) rolls weather on a map that isn't a
planet, such as an event map; it refuses planet layers, which follow their own weather.

Lightning strikes only near a living player who isn't in a container, on an open tile of that player's own grid,
never a station's grid or a vessel, and shocks only mobs standing in the open on it. Every bolt draws the weather's
`bolt` (`WFThunderbolt`): a strike spawns it where it lands, and for a far flash each client draws `WFThunderboltFar`
on open ground somewhere in its own view (`WolfThunderboltSystem`). Someone inside a vehicle's interior is under the sky
of the map the hull is on (`SharedWolfWeatherSystem.GetSkyMap`): they hear its weather loops and thunder, always as from
indoors, and are sent the snow around the hull. Wind pushes only loose light items
that are out in the open, not creatures, buckled or stowed, and only where its own grid goes on downwind, at most
`pushMaxPerTick` items a pass.

Entry points: `SharedWolfWeatherSystem.SetWeather`, `WFPlanetWeatherSystem.ResolveWolfWeather`,
`WeatherScheduleSystem.SetSchedule`, `WolfLightningSystem.FindStrikeTile`, `WindPushSystem.PushAround`, the
`wolfWeather`, `wolfWeatherSettings` and `weatherSchedule` prototypes.
