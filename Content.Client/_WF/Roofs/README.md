# Roofs

Roofs seen from above. Nobody sees a roof on their own level: on the ground beside a building or inside it, aboard a
ship, on a station or in the ship previewer, rooms are seen top-down as always. Only someone on a higher level of the
same z-stack, such as a planet's air layer, a city's upper level or a rooftop, sees the buildings and ships on the levels
below them roofed over: the open floor of every roofed tile gets a solid roof, lit by that level's daylight and shaded
along its edges. A vessel or station's enclosed rooms are roofed as hull plating (exterior plating, catwalks and lattice
open to the outside stay bare), a planet building's roofed floor as roofing. Walls, windows, doors and other
full-height structures are never covered, so from above a building shows its walls and doors with roof only over its
rooms. What stands on each tile is remembered and looked at again whenever the tile or anything anchored on it
changes, so building, deconstruction and breaches show at once. Caverns and other layers under a world layer are left
alone, as their roof is the ground above. A ship climbing or sinking between two levels counts as below the viewer
once it is lower than the floor of the viewer's level.

The z-level renderer draws each lower level through an eye of its own, offset and shrunk for its depth
(`WFPlanetView`), and runs this overlay in that pass, so the roof lands on that level's tiles at the same scale with no
sums of its own. A ghost, an admin ghost or an observer sees no roofs, nor the roof shade that keeps daylight out of
buildings and ships (upstream `RoofOverlay`); underground layers keep their shade.

Entry points: `WFRoofSystem` (which levels are below the viewer and what is covered), `WFRoofTiles` (the coverage
rules), `WFRoofOverlay` (the drawing).
