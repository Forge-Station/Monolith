# VehicleShop

Vehicle purchase terminals. A terminal (`WFVehicleShopComponent`) sells the vehicles of its catalogue
(`wfVehicleCatalog`, `Resources/Prototypes/_WF/VehicleShop/catalogs.yml`) onto the nearest delivery pad
(`WFVehicleDeliveryPad`) on its grid within 16 metres. The window lists each vehicle with a picture and its price; Buy
takes the price from the customer's bank and delivers the vehicle onto the pad, if the pad is clear: nothing solid in the
room the biggest vehicle of the catalogue takes up (loose items and what lies under the floor don't count), and the
window names whatever is in the way. A vehicle parked on the pad is sold back from the same window for 75% of the lower of its appraised value
and its listed price, paid into the bank (as cash for an Ironman); a vehicle with anyone or anything dragged still inside
can't be sold. Mappers place a terminal and a pad with room for the biggest vehicle round it.

The first terminal is `WFComputerVehicleShopExpeditionary` with `WFVehicleCatalogExpeditionary`: two small cargo trucks,
the box and pizza vans, the ambulance and the transport and medical humvees. Prices sit about a fifth over each
vehicle's appraisal, so nothing bought here sells for a profit, here or with a ship (`VehicleShopTest` checks every
catalogue). Entry point: `WFVehicleShopSystem`; the client window is `WFVehicleShopWindow`.

A pad and its terminal are also the repair and upgrade bay. A damaged vehicle on the pad gets a Repair button: the frame
and every part made whole for 20% of its value at worst, less for less damage, 100 at the least (`RepairPrice`). The
expeditionary terminal also lists `WFVehicleCatalogBayParts`, the parts that need no licence, handed over the counter and
fitted with a crowbar on the vehicle as anywhere else. Ships map `WFComputerVehicleShopExpeditionary` and a pad; players buy
`WFVehicleBayTerminalFlatpack` and `WFVehicleDeliveryPadFlatpack` from John Wolfgate and the Wolfgate vendor. The flatpack's
terminal (`WFComputerVehicleBay`) comes apart like any computer, and a pad breaks up into steel.

A terminal can list further catalogues after its own (`extraCatalogs`). A listing that is an item (a part, a beacon, a
licence) is handed over the counter and needs no pad; a terminal that sells only items shows no pad line. A listing
marked `licence: true` is refused to anyone not carrying a heavy vehicle licence (`WFVehicleShopLicenceCheckEvent`,
answered by MilitaryVehicles), and everything sold raises `WFVehicleShopPurchasedEvent` on what was handed over.

Delivery beacons (`WFVehicleBeaconSystem`, `beacons.yml`) are a vehicle bought to carry: used in hand, the beacon is set
down and opens ten seconds later into its vehicle, square to the ground, if nothing solid is in its room (the pad's
check), with the key handed to whoever set it off; blocked, it says what is in the way and can be set off again. A
beacon sells at its catalogue's listing and resells for what the pad would pay for the vehicle. The roadside mechanic
sells them from `WFVehicleCatalogMechanic`: the ATV, two small trucks and two vans. The vehicle repair box
(`WFVehicleRepairKitSystem`) is used on a damaged civilian vehicle (appraised up to 40,000) and makes the frame and
every fitted part whole, once.
